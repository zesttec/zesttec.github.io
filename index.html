I am a PhD candidate in Computer Science at Indiana University Bloomington, advised by Xiaofeng Wang and Haixu Tang. I work closely with Hang Zhang. My research focuses on systems security and privacy, with an emphasis on the security of connected devices, cloud services, and networked systems.

My work spans IoT access control and delegation, voice-assistant privacy, residential proxy measurement, and security analysis with machine learning and language models. Alongside research, I teach systems programming and network security through hands-on practice, clear reasoning, and timely feedback.

arXiv preprint · 2024

Shining Light into the Tunnel: Understanding and Classifying Network Traffic of Residential Proxies

Ronghong Huang, Dongfang Zhao, Xianghang Mi, Xiaofeng Wang

This work develops tools to collect and analyze traffic carried by residential proxy services. It combines distributed traffic collection, analysis of suspicious flows, and machine learning classifiers that identify residential proxy traffic. The resulting framework supports measurement of these services and detection of their traffic in networks.

Residential proxies · Network measurement · Machine learning

Inside the classroom

01

Active learning

Short questions, polls, live coding, and guided exercises make students’ reasoning visible and help me adjust the pace of a class.

02

Structured practice

Incremental assignments begin with a working foundation and build toward deeper understanding. Students practice Git, testing, debugging, and clear documentation.

03

Useful feedback

Clear rubrics distinguish correctness, design, and communication. Frequent checkpoints give students time to apply feedback before major assessments.

04

An accessible classroom

Consistent policies, clear expectations, accommodations, and multiple ways to ask for help support students with different backgrounds and preparation.
